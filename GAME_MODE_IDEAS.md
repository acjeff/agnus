# Game Mode Ideas for PATTRN

Brainstormed ideas for new game modes, building on the existing Easy, Medium, Hard, and Blind modes.

---

## 1. Daily Challenge

A single shared puzzle each day, the same for every player worldwide.

- **Grid:** Varies daily (cycles through 5x5, 7x7, and mixed rules)
- **Attempts:** 5 (standard)
- **Key mechanic:** One shot per day. A global seed based on the calendar date generates the puzzle deterministically. Players can share their result emoji grids (like Wordle). A streak counter tracks consecutive days solved.
- **Why it's fun:** Social competition, daily ritual, "did you get today's PATTRN?" conversations.

---

## 2. Speed Run

Solve a gauntlet of puzzles as fast as possible.

- **Grid:** 5x5 paired (accessible difficulty)
- **Attempts:** 1 per puzzle (must get it right first try or it's over)
- **Key mechanic:** A rolling sequence of puzzles, timer always running. One wrong submission ends the run. Score = number of puzzles solved. Leaderboard tracks best runs.
- **Why it's fun:** High-stakes tension, tests pattern recognition speed under pressure, replayable.

---

## 3. Zen / Endless

Relaxed, infinite play with no failure state.

- **Grid:** Player-selectable (5x5 or 7x7)
- **Attempts:** Unlimited
- **Key mechanic:** No timer, no attempt counter, no score. Puzzles generate endlessly from a random seed. Wrong guesses gently highlight without penalty. Optional soft ambient background color shifts.
- **Why it's fun:** Low-pressure mode for unwinding. Lets players practice pattern recognition without anxiety.

---

## 4. Cascade

Solve a chain of increasingly difficult puzzles without resetting.

- **Grid:** Starts 3x3, grows to 5x5, then 7x7, then 9x9
- **Attempts:** 3 total across ALL puzzles (shared life pool)
- **Key mechanic:** Each solved puzzle adds one row and one column. The pattern type also escalates (paired -> mixed -> independent). Three wrong submissions across the entire cascade and you're out. Score = largest grid completed.
- **Why it's fun:** Escalating challenge with mounting pressure. The grid literally growing feels rewarding and intimidating at the same time.

---

## 5. Fog of War

The grid is partially hidden and reveals itself as you solve.

- **Grid:** 7x7
- **Attempts:** 5
- **Key mechanic:** Only a small region (3x3 window) of filled cells is visible at first. Each correct cell placement reveals adjacent hidden cells. Players must infer the global pattern from limited local information, expanding their view as they go.
- **Why it's fun:** Adds spatial deduction on top of pattern recognition. Each correct guess literally illuminates more of the puzzle, creating satisfying "aha" moments.

---

## 6. Shifter

The pattern rule changes mid-puzzle.

- **Grid:** 7x7
- **Attempts:** 6
- **Key mechanic:** The grid is generated by blending two different generators, split along a boundary (e.g., top half is horizontal stripes, bottom half is checkerboard). The blanks span both regions. Players must recognize that two rules coexist and figure out where each applies.
- **Why it's fun:** Subverts expectations. Just when you think you've cracked the pattern, the other half doesn't fit. Forces flexible thinking.

---

## 7. Mimic

Given a completed reference grid, reproduce the pattern on a blank grid from memory.

- **Grid:** 5x5
- **Attempts:** 3
- **Key mechanic:** A fully solved pattern grid is shown for a limited time (e.g., 5-10 seconds), then hidden. The player must reconstruct the entire grid from memory. Shorter study times = higher score multiplier. No feedback until full submission.
- **Why it's fun:** Tests visual memory alongside pattern recognition. The time pressure during the study phase creates a different kind of tension than the solving phase.

---

## 8. Inverse

Given a set of tiles and a target pattern, figure out the *rule*.

- **Grid:** 5x5, fully filled (no blanks)
- **Attempts:** N/A (multiple choice)
- **Key mechanic:** The grid is shown complete. The player must choose which pattern generator was used from a set of 4 options (e.g., "Checkerboard", "Diagonal Stripes", "Concentric Squares", "Pinwheel"). A series of 20 rounds, scored by accuracy and speed.
- **Why it's fun:** Flips the core mechanic. Instead of filling gaps, you're naming the rule. Builds deeper understanding of the pattern generators and makes players feel like pattern experts.

---

## 9. Duel (2-Player)

Two players race to solve the same puzzle.

- **Grid:** Same puzzle for both players (5x5 or 7x7)
- **Attempts:** 5 each, independent
- **Key mechanic:** Split-screen or turn-based on a single device. Both players see the same puzzle. First to solve wins. If both solve in the same number of attempts, faster time wins. Could also work as async challenges sent between friends via share codes.
- **Why it's fun:** Direct competition. Adds a social/multiplayer dimension to an otherwise solo game.

---

## 10. Mutator

Classic mode but with random rule-bending modifiers each puzzle.

- **Grid:** 7x7
- **Attempts:** 5
- **Key mechanic:** Each puzzle comes with 1-2 random mutators drawn from a pool:
  - *Monochrome:* All colors are the same, pattern is shape-only
  - *Shapeless:* All shapes are the same, pattern is color-only
  - *Rotated:* The grid is displayed rotated 90/180/270 degrees
  - *Inverted:* Filled cells are blank and blank cells are filled
  - *Tiny Window:* Only a 3x3 region of clues is visible (like a mini Fog of War)
  - *No Palette:* Tile options include decoy tiles not in the puzzle
- **Why it's fun:** Every puzzle feels fresh. Mutators force players to adapt their solving strategy each time, preventing autopilot.
